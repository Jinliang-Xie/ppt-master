__When fuel\-cell product water and recovered platinum become resources__

## Abstract

Hydrogen fuel cells generate product water during operation and leave platinum\-rich components at retirement. Yet sustainability assessments often stop at generation or recovery, before either material has changed real resource use. We argue that resource value is realized in use. Product water matters when a compatible local demand receives it at the right quality and time; recovered platinum matters when it enters a qualified product and replaces primary metal. These materials follow opposite clocks. Water's opportunity is immediate and spatially constrained, whereas platinum's is delayed by product lifetimes, collection, qualification and industrial adoption. Evidence from measured water capture, a named non\-potable use, end\-of\-life platinum re\-entry and recycling economics shows that the decisive step lies beyond the device or refinery. Following each material to its destination reveals where resource value is created, where it is lost and how fuel\-cell systems could be designed around real users and secondary\-material markets. This Perspective reframes circularity from the quantity generated or recovered to the primary resource use that actually changes.

# When matter becomes a resource

One material is routinely overlooked; the other is routinely celebrated. Water leaves a fuel\-cell system and often disappears from the sustainability story before meeting a user. Platinum leaves a retired stack and can acquire the language of circularity before entering another product. These opposite habits produce the same mistake. They confuse the presence of matter with the realization of value.

Both materials are compelling because their physical presence is easy to see: water appears at the stack and platinum remains after retirement. But a chemical balance or metallurgical yield says only what exists, not what happens next.

This distinction matters because sustainability is a claim about changed consequences. A litre of condensate that runs to drain has not reduced another water withdrawal. A gram of platinum in a refinery output has not necessarily changed the platinum recipe of any product. Material accounting can reveal an opportunity, but opportunity is not an outcome. The decisive movement occurs when a material enters a situated practice and changes an input that would otherwise have been required.

Hydrogen fuel cells make this problem unusually visible because operation creates one potentially useful output while retirement releases another valuable material. This Perspective follows both beyond the device: product water towards a user, and platinum towards a second product.

Water and platinum belong in one Perspective because they expose the same premature endpoint from opposite temporal directions. Water is a fleeting local flow; platinum is a durable industrial stock. Their contrast asks a common question: when does matter begin to change resource use?

Resources are often described as if usefulness resides inside matter. In practice, usefulness is made by relationships. A stream becomes irrigation water because its chemistry, volume and timing meet the needs of a crop and a place. A secondary metal becomes catalyst input because its composition, morphology, reliability and documentation meet a manufacturer's requirements. The same material can be a resource in one setting and a burden in another.

This relational view clarifies why location matters without turning the article into a geography survey. A nearby user can make a modest water stream valuable by removing transport and storage. A distant user may not. A dense population of retiring stacks can support aggregation. A sparse population can leave valuable platinum dispersed in units that are expensive to collect. Space does not confer value by itself, but it brings materials and uses close enough for value to be realized.

Time performs a similar role. A user who needs water tomorrow cannot benefit from water produced next season. A platinum purchaser, by contrast, may wait for enough retired material to accumulate, provided the material remains identifiable and recoverable. Time can destroy the opportunity for one material and create scale for the other. The contrast is not a metaphor pasted onto two unrelated topics. It follows from how quickly each material must encounter the practice that makes it useful.

Quality mediates both space and time. Water can deteriorate during storage or acquire contaminants from hardware. Platinum can remain elemental platinum while losing the particle size, support structure or manufacturing consistency needed for a particular catalyst. “Recovered” therefore describes an origin and a physical operation. It does not describe fitness for every destination.

The relevant destination need not be the original one. Demanding a perfectly closed loop can mistake symbolism for benefit. Product water returned to electrolysis is intuitively satisfying, but water used for a nearby non\-potable demand may avoid a greater local burden. Platinum recovered from a fuel\-cell stack may deliver a credible substitution in another qualified product even if fuel\-cell re\-entry is not yet practical. The environmental question concerns avoided primary provision, not the visual neatness of the loop.

Three ideas organize the argument: matter can exist without being used; use can occur without displacing primary supply; and substitution can occur without yielding a net sustainability benefit. These are different scientific claims, not a single label for circularity.

Substitution introduces the counterfactual that resource language often avoids. If product water is used, which external water source is used less? If secondary platinum enters a catalyst, which primary platinum input is reduced? Without that comparison, use may be genuine but additive. A new use can create value without reducing an existing burden, just as recovered metal can expand total material supply without displacing mining.

Net value depends on what the new pathway adds. Condensation, treatment and storage consume resources, as do collection, refining, qualification and remanufacture. The relevant comparison is the burden avoided by substitution minus the burden introduced to realize it.

Open\-loop use can be valuable, but the claim follows the material to its destination: water used for vehicle washing offsets that water source, and platinum used in another product belongs to that product's material balance.

This is where product\-level and market\-wide substitution diverge. At product level, a manufacturer can document that a defined secondary mass replaced primary platinum that would otherwise have entered a product. This is a tangible purchasing and material\-balance claim. At market level, the question is whether primary extraction or supply falls across a region or market. Prices, inventories, demand growth and other uses can absorb the difference. The first result does not logically contain the second \[9,10\].

The same distinction applies to water, although the market mechanisms differ. A site may reduce municipal or groundwater demand by using product water. That local substitution can be meaningful even if regional water withdrawals do not visibly change. Leakage, reallocations or growth elsewhere may alter the wider consequence. A bounded site\-level claim should not be dismissed because it is not regional, but it should not be enlarged without evidence.

This view changes how incomplete evidence is read. A low capture rate is not merely a disappointing efficiency number; it shows that most theoretical water never became available to a user under those conditions. A failed recycled\-catalyst durability test is not evidence that recovery is pointless; it identifies qualification as the present limit. Negative findings become intellectually useful because they locate the distance between material and value.

Following materials to users and purchasers also restores the actors who create resource value. Circularity is not completed by mass flow alone; it is completed through decisions about acceptance, specification and use.

### Box 1 | Three meanings that should not be collapsed

__Material existence.__ A physical flow is generated, captured, collected or recovered. Fuel\-cell reaction water exists even when it remains vapour or is discharged. Recovered platinum exists even when only refinery output is known. Existence establishes availability or potential, not use.

__Resource identity.__ The material enters a real use that accepts its quality, quantity, timing and form. Water supplied to a named carwash can have resource identity without being potable or electrolyser\-ready. Secondary platinum has resource identity when a manufacturer or other user accepts it into a qualified material or product.

__Net sustainability benefit.__ The actual use displaces a specified primary input, and the added burdens remain below the burdens avoided. The claim is bounded to the displaced water source or product input. Product\-level platinum substitution does not by itself establish a market\-wide fall in primary extraction.

# Water's short clock: from reaction to user

Fuel\-cell water begins with a striking equivalence. Consuming 1 kg of hydrogen forms about 9 kg of water. The relation is fixed by chemistry, which makes the resulting quantity feel firm. Yet the firmness belongs to the reaction balance. It does not extend automatically to liquid capture, accepted quality, convenient timing or avoided water supply.

Water can leave a proton\-exchange\-membrane fuel cell as vapour, droplets or liquid mixed with condensate from inlet humidity. Membranes, plates, plumbing and ambient air can contribute species relevant to a proposed use. Condensation also changes heat rejection, pressure drop, equipment mass, energy demand and maintenance. Thus, the first material transition is not from hydrogen to useful water. It is from theoretical reaction water to a smaller and differently characterized recoverable stream.

A six\-month study of a commercial 1 kW PEMFC makes the gap concrete. At the reported operating condition, theoretical reaction water was about 402 ml h−1, but passive drain collection averaged 30 ± 5.73 ml h−1, approximately 8%, without active condensation \[11\]. The study also reported concerns involving nickel and aluminium. No treatment, storage or user was part of the observation. The result is specific to that unit and arrangement, but it punctures the intuition that reaction water is already a supply.

A specialized design shows that much higher recovery may be technically imaginable. A model of an air\-cooled portable PEMFC with semi\-closed cathode circulation and internal sodium\-borohydride hydrolysis yielded a multi\-objective optimum of 94.70% water recovery \[12\]. Selected condenser behavior was checked experimentally, but integrated long\-duration recovery was not measured. This is modelled performance for a particular architecture, not an observation of ordinary exhaust capture or external use.

Together, these studies show how strongly system design shapes recoverable water. The passive commercial observation and the optimized semi\-closed model answer different questions and should be read as contrasting architectures rather than a single performance range.

Capture is only the first encounter with reality. The next is quality. Water suitable for vehicle washing, cooling, irrigation, drinking or electrolysis faces different chemical, microbial and reliability requirements. A named PEM\-electrolyser series, for example, specifies at least ASTM Type II deionized feed below 1 µS cm−1 and above 1 MΩ cm \[13\]. This requirement is not universal, but it makes one point unavoidable: fuel\-cell condensate cannot be presumed electrolyser\-ready.

Treatment can widen the set of possible users, but it also alters the comparison. Filters, ion exchange, membranes, disinfection or other polishing steps can consume energy and materials and produce reject streams \[14,15\]. Storage can preserve temporal flexibility while risking contamination or loss. Reserve water may still be needed when the fuel cell is idle or the recovered stream is off\-specification. These are not minor deductions from a gross water credit. They define the water that a user can rely upon.

The identity of the displaced source now becomes decisive. One cubic metre can carry very different significance depending on whether it replaces scarce groundwater, desalinated water, highly treated municipal supply or an abundant low\-burden source. Regional water constraints can matter even when global quantities seem modest \[5\]. Product water has no context\-free sustainability value because both scarcity and provision burdens are situated.

The most important move is from possible use to a named user. Toyota and FuelCell Energy report that the Long Beach Tri\-gen installation routes product water from a stationary molten\-carbonate system to vehicle washing \[16,17\]. The reported maxima are 1,400 US gallons per day and about 0.5 million gallons per year of avoided municipal demand. This adjacent fuel\-cell case is not hydrogen PEMFC evidence. Its value is conceptual and practical: it shows a terrestrial, non\-potable demand attached to operating equipment.

The same site record also reveals what remains invisible: the published material does not provide a repeated generation\-to\-use balance, treatment sequence, quality distribution or auxiliary energy demand. Its significance lies in naming a user and a displaced municipal supply, not in establishing a transferable water credit.

Time matching can be more restrictive than annual volume. A fuel cell used for emergency backup might produce most of its water during rare outages, while a nearby process needs steady daily supply. A mobile fuel cell can produce water along a route far from any practical collection point. Even continuous stationary operation can mismatch seasonal demand. Annual totals conceal the moment when a user can either accept the water or cannot.

Storage can extend the moment of use, but it changes the system through tanks, pumping, maintenance and quality risk. Beyond some distance or duration, the recovered stream loses its local advantage.

Co\-location is therefore an opportunity, not a guarantee. A fuel cell beside a continuous, quality\-compatible non\-potable demand may turn water into a resource with little storage or transport. The same device beside an intermittent or highly purified demand may not. A co\-located electrolyser is an especially demanding receiver because feed quality can be strict and because the fuel cell and electrolyser may operate at different times \[13,15\]. The visual appeal of returning water to hydrogen production should not outrun these conditions.

The stoichiometric symmetry remains useful as a thought experiment: an electrolyser and fuel cell can create equal annual water quantities on paper, yet the practical return is governed by capture, treatment and time\-matched demand. The full 1 MW calculation belongs in the Supplementary Information, where it can illuminate this contrast without becoming the article's main claim.

Figure 2 places the evidence side by side without forcing it onto one scale: passive collection in a commercial PEMFC, an optimized model for a specialized architecture and a named non\-potable use in an adjacent fuel\-cell system. Their contrast reveals where water is lost before it becomes a resource.

The next advance is not simply a higher recovery percentage, but a record that follows water from reaction through capture and quality to a user whose former supply genuinely falls.

Product water becomes a resource at the user's inlet, not at the cathode outlet. Its benefit begins only when the user's former supply is reduced. The added burdens may then preserve, weaken or reverse that benefit. Water's immediacy makes this demanding: the opportunity must be realized while flow, quality, place and demand coincide.

# Platinum's long clock: from recovery to second use

Platinum presents the opposite temptation. Unlike water, it does not evaporate from the argument when immediate use is absent. Its scarcity and value keep attention focused on recovery. A high metallurgical yield can therefore feel like a completed circularity result even when the path to a new product remains long.

Platinum's value is preserved by durability but fragmented by products, owners and return routes. Lower catalyst loading and longer stack life reduce primary demand while delaying and diluting the feedstock available for recovery.

Before metallurgy, there is collection. A recovery yield normally refers to material that reaches a process. It says little about the share of eligible stacks returned. Ownership models, safety procedures, product diversity, geography, refurbishment and residual value affect what arrives. End\-of\-life recycling rates for platinum\-group metals already vary by region \[10\]. A refinery can be efficient while the system loses most eligible material before processing begins.

Refurbishment, reuse, manufacturing\-scrap recycling and end\-of\-life refining should also remain distinct. Refurbishment can preserve component value and delay material processing. Manufacturing scrap is relatively homogeneous and available immediately. Retired stacks are heterogeneous and arrive years after manufacture. Pooling these streams under one recovery percentage hides both their different burdens and their different meanings.

Once platinum is recovered, elemental identity is still not product qualification. Catalyst performance depends on more than the presence of platinum. Composition, particle characteristics, support, dispersion, coating quality and manufacturing consistency can matter. A secondary material can be chemically recoverable yet unsuitable for a particular component. Conversely, it may be suitable for another product. Qualification decides where the recovered platinum can credibly go.

BEST4Hy offers a clear public demonstration of end\-of\-life platinum re\-entry. The consortium reports that all catalyst platinum used in five experimental membrane\-electrode assemblies came from an end\-of\-life EKPO donor stack \[22\]. This denominator concerns the origin of platinum in those five assemblies. It is not a 100% recovery yield from the donor stack, whose recovered mass and recovery yield were not reported.

The five experimental assemblies were tested beside five commercial references in a ten\-cell short stack. Their reported voltage averaged about 92% of the reference group, but coating defects, faster degradation and early termination near 200 h of a planned 500 h left qualification incomplete \[22\].

This is more than metallurgical recovery: end\-of\-life platinum re\-entered a new electrochemical device. It is less than commercial substitution: long durability, routine manufacturing and primary\-platinum purchasing were not established. The case is valuable because it locates the material's present industrial frontier.

Economics can halt the journey even when chemistry succeeds. A Honda\-funded study dismantled three 103 kW Honda Clarity stacks and scaled laboratory processing into 25\-year techno\-economic cases \[23\]. All modeled routes had negative net present value at 1,000 stacks per year. Selected innovative hydrometallurgical routes became positive at 10,000 stacks per year under the study assumptions, while conventional routes required more volume.

Scale changes the industrial opportunity. Under the study assumptions, all routes remained negative at 1,000 stacks per year, whereas selected hydrometallurgical routes became positive at 10,000. The important result is not a universal threshold, but the dependence of recycling economics on the timing and concentration of retired stock.

Environmental burdens can also reverse the apparent gain. Evidence from spent\-catalyst recovery supports this concern for critical metals \[7\]; work on Cu, Au and Ag recovery from circuit boards supplies only a process\-level hydrometallurgical analogy, not platinum\-specific evidence \[8\]. Energy, chemicals, neutralization, waste treatment and electricity mix all matter. High yield is valuable, but yield alone cannot show that secondary platinum carries a lower burden than the primary input it replaces. A net claim requires both the avoided primary provision and the added secondary pathway to be compared on the same terms.

The destination need not be another fuel\-cell stack. An open\-loop use can realize resource value when recovered platinum enters a qualified product and replaces primary platinum there. Requiring the same application can forgo a better use. Yet the claim must move with the material: substitution in another product cannot be presented as fuel\-cell circularity, and open\-market recovery without a known destination remains only potential substitution.

The clearest endpoint is a traceable secondary mass entering a specified product and reducing the primary platinum that product would otherwise require. Performance and durability remain part of the product claim, while market\-wide mining effects require a separate economic analysis.

Water and platinum therefore reveal a common mistake through opposite temporal paths: one opportunity can disappear before it is counted, while the other can be counted before it is realized.

Figure 3 follows platinum from retired stack to experimental re\-entry and scale\-dependent economics. By keeping these records separate, the figure shows both the progress already achieved and the industrial distance that remains.

Platinum becomes a resource again when a qualified user accepts it. It begins to support a sustainability claim when that acceptance replaces specified primary input and survives an added\-burden comparison. The delayed clock grants time to build collection, knowledge and scale. It also creates room for promised circularity to remain indefinitely in the future.

# Two clocks, one resource problem

The water and platinum stories now meet, but they do not merge. Both materials pass from existence to use, substitution and possible net benefit. Their opportunities unfold at different speeds. The immediate spatial water clock and the delayed industrial platinum clock explain why the same conceptual discipline produces different practical questions.

Water's clock starts while the fuel cell operates. Vapour and liquid emerge at a location, with a quality profile and a rate. Unless capture and a compatible demand coincide, the opportunity contracts quickly. Water can be stored or transported, but those acts add equipment, energy, time and risk. Its resource value is perishable even when the molecule itself remains present.

Platinum's clock starts much earlier in one sense and much later in another. Design choices determine initial loading and eventual recoverable mass. Actual recovery waits for retirement. The metal can then survive collection delays and multiple processing stages. Its material durability slows physical loss, while industrial qualification and market adoption determine whether value is realized.

The clocks therefore create opposite errors. Water is discounted because it lacks an immediate user, even in places where modest, well\-matched use could matter. Platinum is credited because future recovery appears plausible, even when collection, qualification and purchasing are unobserved. One potential benefit is lost from sight too early; the other is counted too early.

Spatial form follows these temporal differences. A fuel cell beside steady non\-potable demand may make water useful without transport. A dense regional population of retired stacks may make platinum aggregation economic. A sparse deployment can still create locally useful water during operation while producing too little end\-of\-life feed years later. The same installation can sit in favorable conditions for one resource and unfavorable conditions for the other.

Scale also points in opposite directions. Increasing operating hours can raise the continuous water flow available to a local user, although only if demand matches. Increasing installed stock can eventually raise platinum returns, although only after retirement. A large young fleet may produce abundant water now and almost no retired platinum. A mature declining fleet may do the reverse.

Technology improvement complicates the clocks further. Better water capture could widen the immediate opportunity but add energy or maintenance. Longer stack life and lower platinum loading can reduce primary demand while delaying and shrinking secondary feed. A result that weakens a recycling business can still improve material efficiency. Circularity should not be allowed to turn recovery volume into the sole measure of progress.

Figure 4 turns the asymmetry into a map of opportunity. Water value grows where regular operation, capture, quality and nearby demand coincide; platinum value grows where retired stock, collection, qualification and purchasing converge. The two landscapes need not peak in the same project or at the same time.

They also suggest different kinds of patience. Water studies need fine temporal resolution because annual averages can invent a match that never occurs. Platinum studies need long institutional memory because retirement, ownership and procurement cross project and organizational boundaries. One needs to observe the fleeting encounter; the other needs to preserve traceability across delay.

A single circularity score would erase these differences. Water quantity and platinum recovery percentage use different denominators and clocks; combining them can reward systems with neither a water user nor a qualified secondary product.

This question returns resource value to lived systems. A carwash operator, water manager, dismantler, refiner, catalyst maker and purchaser appear as part of the scientific explanation. Their decisions are not noise around a material flow. They are the means by which matter becomes useful. Circularity is thus less a property of the stack than an accomplishment distributed across time, place and institutions.

# When resource value matters

The two resource streams will not dominate every fuel\-cell decision. Their importance rises when they alter a scarce local input, an industrial capability or the scale at which a system can operate.

Product water is most likely to matter where operation is regular, capture is practical and a nearby demand accepts the water quality. Timing must also match, and the displaced source must carry appreciable scarcity or treatment burden. Its value may be small where the device rarely operates or vapour is difficult to condense. Distant users, stringent quality requirements and abundant low\-burden external water can reduce it further. These conditions concern the destination, not the prestige of the fuel\-cell application.

Recovered platinum is most likely to matter where eligible products are returned and enough feed accumulates for viable processing. The material must meet a valuable specification, manufacturers must accept it and primary platinum use must correspondingly fall. Its value may be delayed or small where ownership is fragmented or stacks are long\-lived and dispersed. Low platinum loading, failed qualification or demand growth faster than secondary supply can weaken it further.

These judgments can move in opposite directions within the same project. A long\-lived stationary system beside a suitable water demand may realize a water benefit while postponing a small platinum return. A mature fleet with organized take\-back may support platinum aggregation even if its mobile water stream has no practical user. There is no reason to expect both resource opportunities to rise together.

These opportunities remain secondary to the service itself and the burden of hydrogen supply. They matter after a fuel\-cell role is credible, and they can still change where, how or at what scale that role is pursued.

The reverse mistake is also possible. Because water quantities or recovered platinum may be small relative to total system burdens, analysts may omit them entirely. That can hide locally important substitutions and miss design opportunities. A modest water stream can matter at a constrained site. A small qualified secondary\-platinum flow can establish a new industrial capability. Magnitude should be assessed, not presumed from global totals.

Figure 4 brings these conditions together: water value rises with local stress, proximity and time matching; platinum value rises with mature return volume, qualification and adoption. Either opportunity can matter alone, together or not at all.

The decisive next studies should follow one material continuously to its destination: a PEMFC water stream to a named user and displaced water source, or an end\-of\-life platinum flow to a qualified product and reduced primary input. Such records would turn today's fragments into observed resource relations.

These observations would allow failure to teach. Water that remains off\-specification can reveal a poor destination or a need for different capture design. Recycled catalyst that fails durability can redirect material toward another qualified use. Return volume that remains uneconomic can motivate aggregation, design for disassembly or a different ownership arrangement. Negative results identify the boundary of value more honestly than generic circularity promises.

This inversion has implications beyond fuel cells. Many sustainability claims begin from visible flows: captured carbon, recovered heat, recycled polymers, reclaimed nutrients or repurposed batteries. In each case, physical availability can be mistaken for realized value. The present evidence is specific to fuel\-cell water and platinum, so broader application remains an analogy. The underlying discipline is portable: ask what use occurred, what input changed and what burdens were added.

The unresolved question is no longer whether water can be captured or platinum recovered. It is how often these materials reach uses that change primary supply, and whether those changes are large enough to shape system design.

This gap reflects the organization of research itself: electrochemistry stops at generation, separation science at recovery, engineering at prototype performance and economics at modeled scale. Resource value appears in the connections between these domains.

## Conclusion: from material flow to realized value

Resource value is not stored inside a molecule or a metal atom. It arises in a relation among matter, a user, a place and time, and an input that no longer needs to be supplied. Product water and recovered platinum expose this because one relation expires quickly while the other can be promised indefinitely.

Water asks whether an encounter happened in time: did a usable flow reach someone who needed it before the opportunity disappeared? Platinum asks whether institutions sustained a chain across years: did owners return it, processors recover it, manufacturers qualify it and purchasers choose it? The clocks are different because the relations that create value are different.

Product\-level substitution and market\-wide extraction remain distinct scales. A manufacturer can reduce primary platinum in one product even when aggregate mining does not yet fall.

The most consequential future evidence may look modest: one PEMFC water stream reliably replacing one named local supply, or one end\-of\-life platinum flow entering one qualified product with documented primary\-input reduction. Its importance would lie not in the grandeur of a circular loop, but in showing that the relation actually occurred and mattered at a defensible scale.

Follow the droplet to the user and the platinum to the product. Resource value begins where those destinations change what the world must supply.

# Supplementary Note | Stoichiometric water\-return illustration

Stoichiometry makes the temptation especially vivid. In a 1 MW illustration operated for 4,000 full\-load hours, the electrolyser's theoretical process\-water demand and the fuel cell's theoretical reaction\-water output can both be 692.3 m3 per year. Yet equality at the reaction boundaries is only a symmetry on paper. It does not create a user, preserve liquid water through capture and treatment, or make production and demand occur at the same moment.

The calculation is retained in the Supplementary Information because its conceptual lesson is simple: a balanced equation does not create a balanced place and time. The usable return narrows through liquid capture, treatment, storage and time\-matched acceptance, while upstream electricity water, cooling, purification reject, treatment energy and reserve supply remain outside the neat stoichiometric mirror.

# Figure captions and text\-only storyboards

__Graphical Abstract | Resource value is realized in use.__

Fuel\-cell product water can disappear before reaching a user, whereas recovered platinum can receive circularity credit before reaching a qualified product. The two paths meet at actual use and the primary resource input that use changes. The graphic is conceptual; line widths do not represent quantities.

__Figure 1 | Two resource clocks, one principle.__

Product water follows a short, place\-bound path from generation to capture, accepted quality, a nearby user and reduced external water supply. Recovered platinum follows a longer industrial path from retirement to collection, qualification, product adoption and reduced primary\-platinum input. The timelines are intentionally unequal: water value can expire quickly, whereas platinum value can be delayed for years.

__Figure 2 | Product water from reaction to real use.__

a, Theoretical reaction water and passively collected liquid in a commercial 1 kW PEMFC. b, A modelled high\-recovery result for a specialized semi\-closed architecture, shown separately rather than as the upper end of one empirical range. c, A named non\-potable user in the Long Beach Tri\-gen case. d, The 1 MW stoichiometric return calculation is retained in the Supplementary Information as a sensitivity illustration. Together, the panels show that resource value emerges only after capture, quality, timing and user acceptance align.

__Figure 3 | Recovered platinum from retired stack to second use.__

a, End\-of\-life\-derived platinum entering experimental membrane\-electrode assemblies and a ten\-cell short stack in BEST4Hy. b, Reported relative voltage, test duration and the unresolved donor\-mass denominator shown as distinct observations. c, Scale\-dependent recycling economics for 1,000 and 10,000 stacks per year under the published assumptions. The figure separates technical re\-entry, product qualification and industrial adoption rather than merging them into a single circularity score.

__Figure 4 | When water and platinum become consequential.__

Water value rises with regular operation, practical capture, accepted quality, nearby demand and a consequential external water source. Platinum value rises with mature return volumes, organized collection, qualification and manufacturer adoption. The two landscapes can peak separately: a project may realize one resource opportunity, both or neither.
